# TransRentals — Vendor Platform Features

This document explains, in plain language, everything a vendor (bike/car/bus/truck/equipment/packers-and-movers supplier, etc.) can do once they're registered and approved on TransRentals — what each feature is, and how it actually works day-to-day. It's written so that vendors, managers, and anyone else on the team can read it and come away understanding the vendor experience — no code knowledge needed, and no technical jargon.

It only covers features that are genuinely visible and usable by a vendor today. Features that exist in the code but aren't currently shown to vendors — including a couple that only *look* real but aren't — are called out separately at the end, so nothing gets mistaken for a working feature by accident.

---

## 1. Onboarding & Account Setup

When a vendor signs up, they go through a guided setup before they can start taking bookings:

- **Business Profile** — the vendor fills in their business name, owner details, contact info, and logo, so their identity is set up on the platform.
- **Documents** — the vendor uploads their KYC and business paperwork, one file per document type, and can see each one's status (pending / approved / rejected / resubmit-required). A few documents are required (Address Proof and PAN Card); the rest — Business Registration, Cancelled Cheque, Owner's Aadhaar, GST Certificate — are optional. Uploading the required documents moves the vendor to "Under Review"; TransRentals' team then verifies each one, and final approval to go live is a separate, manual decision made by an admin.
- **Bank Details** — the vendor adds the bank account where their earnings will be paid out.
- **Partnership Agreement** — the vendor reads and digitally accepts TransRentals' terms of partnership before going live.
- **Get Started Guide** — a step-by-step checklist that walks a brand-new vendor through everything above, so nothing gets missed.
- **Services & Locations** — the vendor picks which services they offer (bike rental, car rental, bus, etc.) and which cities/areas they operate in. If they later want to add a new service or a new city, they can request it, and TransRentals' team approves or rejects the request.
- Until a vendor is fully approved, they see a limited version of the dashboard — most features stay locked until their documents and details are reviewed and accepted.

## 2. Service-by-Service Booking Workflow

TransRentals covers 8 different rental categories — **Bike Rentals, Self-Drive Cars, Car Rental (with driver), Luxury Cars, Bus & Tempo Traveller, Packers & Movers, Transporter (trucks/heavy vehicles), and Equipment Rental**. Every one of these works the same way for a vendor:

1. A customer submits a request for that service (e.g. "I need a bike for 3 days in Goa").
2. The vendor sees this as a **new quote request** and responds with their price and terms.
3. If the customer accepts the vendor's quote, it automatically turns into a **confirmed booking** — the vendor doesn't have to do anything extra to convert it.
4. The vendor can track all their bookings for that service, with cancelled ones kept in a separate list so the active list stays clean.
5. The vendor can see exactly how much they've earned from that service, broken down booking by booking.

To save time, most services also let the vendor save **quote templates** — reusable pricing/terms they've written before — instead of typing the same response over and over. Luxury Cars additionally has **Active Trips** tracking, so the vendor can see which of their luxury bookings are currently in progress.

## 3. Fleet Management

This is where a vendor manages the actual vehicles/equipment they own:

- **All Vehicles** — a full list of everything the vendor has registered on the platform.
- **Add Vehicle** — a form to register a new vehicle, with its details and documents.
- **Fleet Documents** — per-vehicle paperwork like the RC (registration certificate), insurance, and permits, so the vendor always knows what's valid and what's expiring.
- **Service & Maintenance** — the vendor logs when each vehicle was last serviced and when the next service is due.
- **Incidents** — if a vehicle is in an accident or has a problem, the vendor logs it here and can track any insurance claim tied to it.
- **Fuel Tracking** — the vendor records fuel expenses per vehicle, useful for understanding running costs.
- **Fleet Analytics** — a summary view showing how the whole fleet is performing (utilization, which vehicles are earning the most, etc.).

## 4. Staff Management

For vendors who employ drivers or other staff:

- **All Staff** — a directory of the vendor's employees.
- **Add Employee** — a form to add a new staff member.
- **Attendance** — the vendor marks who showed up for work each day.
- **Payroll** — salaries are calculated automatically based on the attendance that's been logged, so the vendor doesn't have to do the maths by hand.

## 5. Selling Directly (Outside the Quote Cycle)

Beyond the normal request-a-quote flow, vendors have a few ways to get bookings directly:

- **Direct Bookings** — a vendor can create a booking themselves on behalf of a customer (for example, someone who called them directly), skipping the whole quote-request back-and-forth.
- **Booking Links** — a vendor can generate their own personal booking page (with a QR code), and share it — say, on WhatsApp — so a customer can book straight from that link without going through TransRentals' search at all.
- **Bulk / Event Bookings** — a separate, more detailed workflow for large jobs like weddings, corporate events, or multi-day trips that need more than a single simple booking.

## 6. B2B / Corporate Business

Some bookings come from corporate clients rather than individual customers. Vendors can see **Open Requests** from these corporate clients and pick up jobs, and then track the ones they've taken on under **My Assignments**.

## 7. Wander Tours

"Wander" is TransRentals' separate travel-package marketplace. Vendors who want to sell holiday/tour packages (not just vehicle rentals) can:

- **Create and manage listings** for tour packages (destination, itinerary, pricing).
- Receive and respond to **quote requests** for those tours, and see resulting **bookings**.
- Check **Analytics** — how many people viewed a listing and how many actually booked it.
- See **Reviews** left by travelers who took the tour.

## 8. P2P Marketplace (Vendor-to-Vendor)

If a vendor's vehicle is sitting idle, instead of it earning nothing, they can rent it out to *another* vendor on the platform:

- **Post Vehicle** — offer an idle vehicle for other vendors to rent.
- **Available Vehicles** — browse what other vendors are offering and rent one in.
- **Share Booking** — if a vendor has more customer demand than they can fulfil themselves, they can pass a booking to another vendor instead of turning the customer away.
- **Available Bookings** — browse bookings other vendors are looking to share.
- **My Activity / My Postings** — track what the vendor has posted or picked up through this system.

## 9. Social Network

An internal community feed just for vendors — they can post updates, comment on each other's posts, and view each other's profiles. It's meant as a space for vendors to connect, share tips, and learn from each other, separate from the actual booking/business tools.

## 10. Live GPS Tracking

A live map showing the real-time location of any driver who has location-sharing turned on, so a vendor can see where their vehicles/drivers currently are, all from one screen.

## 11. Financials

A single combined view that pulls together revenue and payments across every service the vendor offers, instead of the vendor having to check each service separately. It shows an overall profit-and-loss picture, a breakdown of earnings per service, and how much is still pending to be paid out.

## 12. Communication

- **Customer Chats** — vendors can message customers directly inside TransRentals, so conversations about a booking don't have to move to WhatsApp or phone calls.
- **Notifications** — alerts for things like a new quote request or a booking confirmation, and a settings page to control which notifications the vendor actually wants to receive.

## 13. Mobile App

Alongside the website, there's also a dedicated **vendor mobile app**. It covers the core day-to-day needs on the go — managing the fleet, checking bookings, chatting with customers, and a verified-vendor badge — so a vendor isn't tied to a desktop to run their business.

## Built, but not currently shown to vendors

These exist in the code — the pages work if you know how to reach them — but there's no link to them in the vendor dashboard menu right now, so an ordinary vendor won't stumble onto them. They fall into three groups:

**✅ Fully working, just hidden from the menu (safe to turn back on):**
- **Calendar** — a real, working calendar view that pulls together every booking across all 8 services (bike, car, bus, truck, luxury, packers, equipment, chauffeur-driven car) plus fleet and staff info, all in one place. This one genuinely works end-to-end and could be switched back on with no risk.

**⚠️ Partly working (needs more backend work before it should be shown):**
- **Help & Support** — the FAQ list and contact details shown here are real and correct. But the "submit a support ticket" form only *looks* like it works — it shows a fake "submitted successfully" message without actually saving or sending the ticket anywhere. This would need to be properly connected before vendors see it.

**❌ Not real yet — just a mockup with made-up numbers (should not be shown until built for real):**
Customer CRM, Leads, standalone Quotations, Invoices & Billing, Expense tracking, Reports & Analytics, a separate Communication hub, Contracts, Daily Checklist, Route Planner, standalone Maintenance/Document Alerts, Goals & Targets, Rate Comparison, Branches, standalone Insurance Claims, Audit Logs, Quick Actions, Toll Management, Fines & Penalties, Driver Training, standalone Customer Feedback, Vendor Wallet, Promotions, standalone Reviews, and Settings. These all display realistic-looking numbers and lists, but none of it is connected to real data — it's placeholder UI built ahead of the backend work, not something a vendor should be shown yet.

If any of these need to go live, the front-end is already there — it's just a matter of prioritizing which ones get real backend support next.
